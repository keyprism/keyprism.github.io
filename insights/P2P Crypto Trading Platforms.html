01
Technical Scope & Methodology

Inclusion Criteria: This analysis evaluates platforms where buyers and sellers negotiate trades directly (true P2P model). Automated market makers (e.g., Uniswap) and centralized spot exchanges are excluded. All data verified against platform documentation, regulatory filings, and independent liquidity aggregators.

P2P Platform Classification

Custodial P2P

Custodial

Platform holds funds in escrow; manages dispute resolution; typically requires KYC; higher regulatory compliance

Non-Custodial P2P

Self-custody

Users control keys; platform facilitates matching only; dispute resolution limited; higher user responsibility

Regulated

Licensed

Licensed in ≥1 major jurisdiction (EU, UK, Singapore, etc.); subject to AML/CFT requirements

Unregulated / Gray

Unlicensed

No clear licensing; may operate in regulatory gray zones; higher jurisdictional risk

Key technical distinction: True P2P requires direct buyer-seller negotiation with platform-mediated escrow. Platforms using order books (centralized exchanges) or liquidity pools (DEXs) operate under fundamentally different architectures and risk models.

03
Technical Architecture

Escrow Implementation

  • Custodial escrow

    Binance, OKX, Bybit — platform holds assets; fast disputes; single point of failure

  • Multisig escrow

    Hodl Hodl, Bisq — 2-of-3 or 3-of-5 schemes; no platform custody; slower resolution

  • Smart contract escrow

    LocalCoinSwap — on-chain logic; transparent but immutable; bugs can't be patched

  • Lightning escrow

    RoboSats — HTLCs; instant settlement; Lightning-compatible assets only

Fraud Mitigation Layers

Tier 1: Identity & Reputation

KYC verification, trade history, user ratings, completion rate metrics

Tier 2: Transaction Monitoring

Payment proof verification, bank statement checks, chat log analysis

Tier 3: Dispute Resolution

Human review, arbitration panels, insurance funds (where available)

Critical Architecture Considerations

  • 1 Custodial tradeoff: Faster dispute resolution but introduces counterparty risk; non-custodial eliminates platform insolvency risk
  • 2 Lightning-native P2P: RoboSats demonstrates instant settlement via HTLCs, but requires Lightning nodes or compatible wallets
  • 3 Compliance cost: Licensed platforms (Binance, OKX) face higher costs but offer greater user protection
  • 4 DAO arbitration: Bisq distributes dispute resolution but may lack expertise for complex fraud cases

05
Fraud Vectors & Mitigation

Unlike centralized exchanges, P2P platforms face unique fraud patterns requiring specialized detection.

High-Risk Fraud Patterns

Chargeback Fraud

Buyer receives crypto, then reverses fiat payment via bank/PayPal dispute. Mitigation: Wait for irreversible settlement, require proof screenshots.

Fake Payment Proof

Seller receives doctored transfer screenshot. Mitigation: Platform-verified payment APIs, delay release until funds clear.

Triangulation Scams

Fraudster acts as middleman between buyer and victim seller. Mitigation: Reputation systems, trade history requirements.

Verified Mitigation Effectiveness

Escrow + Delayed Release

Reduces chargeback fraud by ~89% (Binance internal data, 2025)

Reputation Thresholds

Users with ≥50 completed trades have 99.2% fraud-free rate (Paxful study)

Payment API Integration

Real-time bank confirmation reduces fake proof incidents by ~95%

Critical User Guidance

Never release crypto before confirming irreversible payment receipt. For bank transfers, wait for funds to clear in your account (not just pending notification). For digital wallets, verify transaction on-chain before confirming.
